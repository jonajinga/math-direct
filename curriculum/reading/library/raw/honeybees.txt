# Who Lives in the Hive

A hive is a busy home for thousands of bees. In summer one hive may hold
fifty thousand bees. There are three kinds of bees in a hive. Each kind
of bee has its own work to do.

There is only one queen in the hive. She is the biggest bee in the whole
hive. Her one job is to lay all the eggs. In summer she may lay more
than a thousand eggs a day. The other bees feed her and keep her clean.

Most of the bees in the hive are workers. Workers are all female, like
the queen. A worker's job changes as she gets older. A young worker
cleans the cells and feeds the baby bees. Later she makes wax and builds
the comb. The comb is made of cells with six sides. Older workers guard
the door of the hive. The oldest workers fly out to gather food. A
worker bee lives only about six weeks in summer.

The drones are the male bees of the hive. They are bigger than workers,
with large eyes. Drones do not gather food, and they cannot sting. Their
only job is to mate with a new queen. In fall the workers push the
drones out of the hive.

V: hive = a box or hollow tree where bees live
V: comb = the wax cells where bees keep honey and eggs
V: drone = a male bee
Q: What are the three kinds of bees in a hive? | The three kinds are the queen, the workers, and the drones. | literal
Q: Why do the workers push the drones out in fall? | The drones would eat the hive's food all winter and do no work. | inference
Q: What is a drone? | A drone is a male bee that cannot sting or gather food. | vocabulary
Q: What is this reading mostly about? | A hive has a queen, workers, and drones, each with their own work. | idea

# Nectar, Pollen, and Honey

A worker bee flies out to find flowers. Flowers hold a sweet juice
called nectar deep inside. The bee sucks up the nectar with her long
tongue. She stores it in a special stomach, just for honey. On one trip
a bee may visit a hundred flowers.

Flowers also make a fine yellow dust called pollen. Pollen sticks to the
hairs on the bee's body. The bee brushes it into little baskets on her
back legs. Pollen is food for the baby bees. It is rich food, and it
helps them grow strong.

Back at the hive, the bee passes the nectar to another worker. That bee
passes it on again, from mouth to mouth. Each bee adds a little juice
from her own body. Then the bees put the nectar in a cell of the comb.
They fan it with their wings for hours. The fanning dries the water out
of the nectar. At last the nectar grows thick and sweet. Now it is
honey, and it will keep for years. The bees seal the cell with a cap of
wax.

Honey is the bees' food for winter. A hive may store fifty pounds of it
or more. One bee makes very little honey in her life. All her work makes
about one twelfth of a teaspoon.

V: nectar = the sweet juice inside a flower
V: pollen = the fine yellow dust a flower makes
V: seal = to close up tight
Q: Where does a bee keep the nectar she gathers? | She keeps it in a special stomach made just for honey. | literal
Q: Why does a hive need so many thousands of workers to make honey? | One bee makes very little honey, so it takes many bees. | inference
Q: What is nectar? | Nectar is the sweet juice that a flower holds deep inside. | vocabulary
Q: What is this reading mostly about? | How bees gather nectar and pollen and turn nectar into honey. | idea

# The Dance of the Bees

When a bee finds a good patch of flowers, she flies home. She wants to
tell the other bees where it is. But a bee has no words to say it with.
So she does a dance on the comb instead.

The bee walks in a straight line and waggles her body. Then she turns
and circles back to where she began. The other bees crowd around and
feel her moves. The line she walks points the way to the flowers.
Straight up on the comb means toward the sun. A long waggle means the
flowers are far away. A short waggle means they are close by. A
scientist named Karl von Frisch worked this out.

Bees also do an important job for plants. When a bee goes from flower to
flower, pollen goes with her. Many flowers need pollen from another
flower to make seeds. Without bees, many plants would make no fruit at
all. Apples, pears, berries, and almonds all need bees.

Bees matter to people as well as to plants. They give us honey to eat
and wax for candles. And they help grow much of the food we eat. That is
why people plant flowers for the bees. They also try to keep the bees
safe from harm.

V: patch = a small area of ground
V: waggle = to shake from side to side
V: scientist = a person who studies how things work
Q: What does a long waggle tell the other bees? | A long waggle means the flowers are far from the hive. | literal
Q: Why do the other bees crowd around the dancer? | They need to feel her moves to learn where the flowers are. | inference
Q: What does waggle mean? | To waggle means to shake the body from side to side. | vocabulary
Q: What is this reading mostly about? | Bees dance to share where flowers are, and bees help plants and people. | idea
