# What Is Inside a Seed

A seed is small, but it holds a lot. Inside a seed is a tiny plant,
waiting to grow. It has a little root and a little shoot. The seed also
holds a store of food. The baby plant will eat this food when it starts
to grow.

A hard skin covers the seed on the outside. This skin is called the seed
coat. It keeps the baby plant safe and dry. It keeps out bugs and keeps
the food fresh.

You can see these parts in a bean. Soak a bean in water for one night.
Then slip off the thin coat and pull the bean apart. It splits into two
fat halves of white food. The tiny plant lies between the halves. It is
pale and curled, and very small.

Seeds come in many shapes and sizes. A poppy seed is as small as a grain
of sand. A coconut holds one of the biggest seeds in the world. A seed
can wait for a long time. Some seeds lie in the ground for many years.
They do not grow until the right time comes.

V: shoot = the first stem that grows up from a seed
V: store = a supply of something kept for later use
V: pale = light in color, almost white
Q: What covers the outside of a seed? | A hard skin called the seed coat covers the outside. | literal
Q: Why must you soak the bean before you pull it apart? | Soaking makes the hard coat soft so it slips off. | inference
Q: What does pale mean? | Pale means light in color, almost white, like the tiny plant. | vocabulary
Q: What is this reading mostly about? | A seed holds a tiny plant, its food, and a hard coat. | idea

# The Seed Wakes Up

A seed needs three things to start to grow. It needs water, warmth, and
air to begin. In spring the rain falls and the ground warms up. That is
the time when most seeds wake up.

First the seed drinks in water through its hard coat. The seed swells
and grows fat with water. Then the seed coat splits open at last. A
little white root pushes out through the crack. The root always grows
down, deep into the soil. It holds the young plant in its place. It also
drinks up water from the wet ground.

Next the green shoot pushes up out of the seed. The shoot always grows
up toward the light. Many shoots are bent at first, like a hook. The
bent hook pushes up through the dirt first. Then the shoot straightens
up in the open air. Small leaves open out and turn green in the
sunlight.

Most seeds do not need any light to sprout. They sprout in the dark
under the ground. But the leaves need light as soon as they open. Leaves
use sunlight to make food for the plant. Now the store of food in the
seed is gone. The young plant does not need it any more. It makes its
own food in the sunlight.

V: sprout = to start to grow from a seed
V: swell = to grow bigger and rounder
V: warmth = a gentle heat
Q: What three things does a seed need to grow? | A seed needs water, warmth, and air to grow. | literal
Q: Why does the root grow out before the shoot? | The plant needs water and a firm hold before it grows up. | inference
Q: What does sprout mean? | To sprout means to start to grow from a seed. | vocabulary
Q: What is this reading mostly about? | How a seed sprouts and sends a root down and a shoot up. | idea

# The Parts of a Plant

A plant has four main parts. They are the roots, the stem, the leaves,
and the flowers. Each part has its own job to do.

The roots grow under the ground. They hold the plant up so the wind
cannot blow it down. They take in water from the soil. The water has
tiny bits of minerals in it. The plant needs these to stay healthy.

The stem holds the leaves up to the light. It also carries water up from
the roots. Inside the stem are tiny tubes, like straws. Put a white
flower in a cup of colored water. In a day its petals start to show the
color. The water went up the tubes in the stem.

The leaves make food for the plant. They use sunlight, air, and water to
do it.

The flowers make new seeds for the plant. Bright petals draw bees to the
flower. The bee gets a yellow dust called pollen on its body. It carries
the pollen to the next flower. Then that flower can make seeds. The
seeds grow inside a fruit, like an apple or a pea pod. The wind or an
animal may carry the seeds away. Where the seeds land, new plants begin
to grow.

V: minerals = tiny bits of rock and salt that plants need
V: petals = the colored parts of a flower
V: pollen = a yellow dust that flowers make to grow seeds
