# The Long Sleep

Winter is a hard time for many animals. The days are short and cold.
Snow covers the ground, and food is hard to find. Some animals get
through winter by sleeping. This deep sleep is called hibernation.

A bear gets ready in the fall. It eats and eats until it is very fat.
Then it finds a den, like a cave or a hollow log. It curls up and sleeps
for months. Its heart beats slowly, only a few times a minute. The bear
does not eat or drink all winter. It lives on the fat it stored in fall.
Some bear cubs are even born in the den in winter. In spring the bear
wakes up, thin and hungry.

The wood frog goes even further. In fall it digs under the leaves on the
forest floor. When the cold comes, the frog freezes hard. Its heart
stops, and it does not breathe at all. Sugar in its body keeps the ice
from harming it. When spring comes, the frog thaws out and hops away.

Some bats sleep through winter, too. They hang upside down in caves,
packed close together. Their bodies grow as cold as the cave around
them. A bat's heart may slow from hundreds of beats a minute to about
ten.

V: hibernation = a deep sleep that lasts all winter
V: den = a hidden home where a wild animal rests
V: thaw = to warm up and melt after being frozen
Q: What does a bear do in the fall to get ready for winter? | It eats and eats until it is very fat. | literal
Q: Why does the bear wake up thin in the spring? | It lived on its fat all winter and did not eat. | inference
Q: What does thaw mean? | To thaw means to warm up and melt after being frozen. | vocabulary
Q: What is this reading mostly about? | Some animals get through winter by sleeping while their bodies slow down. | idea

# The Long Journey

Some animals do not stay for winter at all. They travel to a warmer
place where there is food. This long trip is called migration.

Canada geese fly south in the fall. They fly in the shape of a V. The
goose in front stirs the air. That gives a lift to the geese behind it.
The geese take turns at the front. Some fly for more than a thousand
miles. In spring they fly back north to nest.

Monarch butterflies make one of the longest trips of all. In fall they
leave Canada and the northern United States. They fly south to a few
mountains in Mexico. The trip may be nearly three thousand miles. There
the monarchs cover the fir trees by the millions. They rest all winter
in the cool mountain air. In spring they start back north. But no single
butterfly makes the whole round trip. The ones that fly south in fall
were never there before.

Gray whales make a long trip in the sea. In summer they feed in the cold
sea near Alaska. In fall they swim south along the coast to Mexico.
There, in warm bays, the mothers have their calves. The whole trip there
and back is about ten thousand miles.

V: migration = a long trip animals make to a warmer place
V: calves = baby whales or baby cows
V: coast = the land along the edge of the sea
Q: Where do monarch butterflies spend the winter? | They rest on fir trees in the mountains of Mexico. | literal
Q: Why do the geese take turns flying at the front? | The front goose works hardest, so they share the hard job. | inference
Q: What is migration? | Migration is a long trip animals make to find a warmer place. | vocabulary
Q: What is this reading mostly about? | Geese, monarchs, and whales travel far to spend winter somewhere warm. | idea

# Staying and Changing

Many animals stay at home all through the winter. They do not sleep, and
they do not travel. Instead, their bodies or their ways change for the
cold.

The snowshoe hare has a brown coat in summer. In fall, as the days grow
short, its coat turns white. Now it is very hard to see against the
white snow. A fox or an owl may pass right by it. The hare's big back
feet work like snowshoes. They spread its weight so it does not sink.

Deer stay for the winter as well. In fall a deer grows a thick winter
coat. Each hair is hollow and holds warm air inside. The coat keeps the
deer warm even in deep snow. Grass is gone, so deer eat twigs, buds, and
bark. On the coldest days they gather in a sheltered spot. Many deer
together pack down paths in the snow.

Gray squirrels get ready for winter in the fall. Each one buries nuts
and acorns all over the woods. It digs a small hole for each one. In
winter it finds them again by smell, even under snow. It does not find
every nut it buried. The nuts it misses grow into new trees. On very
cold days the squirrel stays curled in its nest of leaves.

V: hare = an animal like a big rabbit with long legs
V: hollow = empty inside
V: sheltered = kept safe from wind and weather
Q: What does a gray squirrel do with nuts in the fall? | It buries them in small holes all over the woods. | literal
Q: Why does a white coat help the hare in winter? | A fox or an owl cannot see it against the snow. | inference
Q: What does hollow mean? | Hollow means empty inside, like a tube or a straw. | vocabulary
Q: What is this reading mostly about? | Some animals stay through winter and change their coats or their ways. | idea
