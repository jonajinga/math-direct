# From Field to Flour

Most of the bread we eat begins in a field of wheat. Wheat is a kind of
grass, and farmers plant it by the acre. In spring the young wheat is
green and soft, like a lawn. By summer the stalks stand tall and turn a
golden brown. At the top of each stalk is a head full of grains. Each
grain is a seed, and inside it is a store of starchy food.

The grain is ready when it is hard and dry. Then it is time for the
harvest to begin. Long ago, people cut the wheat by hand with a curved
blade. They beat the heads with sticks to knock the grains loose. Today
a machine called a combine harvester does all of this at once. It cuts
the stalks, shakes out the grains, and blows away the husks.

The grains go to a mill to be ground into flour. Old mills crushed the
grain between two heavy round stones. A water wheel or a windmill turned
the heavy top stone. Modern mills grind the grain between steel rollers
instead. Whole wheat flour keeps every part of the grain, so it is
brown. White flour has the brown outer coat sifted out. One acre of
wheat can grow enough flour for thousands of loaves.

V: harvest = the gathering of crops when they are ripe
V: combine = a big farm machine that cuts and cleans grain
V: mill = a place where grain is ground into flour
Q: What does a combine do with the wheat? | It cuts the stalks, shakes out the grains, and blows away the husks. | literal
Q: Why did the harvest take much longer long ago? | People cut and beat the wheat by hand, without a machine. | inference
Q: What is a mill? | A mill is a place where grain is ground into flour. | vocabulary
Q: What is this reading mostly about? | Wheat grows in a field, is harvested, and is ground into flour. | idea

# Dough, Yeast, and the Oven

A simple loaf of bread needs only four things. They are flour, water,
salt, and yeast. The baker mixes them into a soft, sticky dough. Then
the baker kneads the dough, pushing and folding it again and again.
Kneading makes the dough smooth and stretchy, so it can hold air.

Yeast is the secret of a light and airy loaf. It is a living thing, so
tiny that one cannot be seen. In warm water the yeast wakes up and
starts to feed. It eats the sugar in the flour and gives off a gas. The
gas forms thousands of tiny bubbles all through the dough. The dough
swells until it is twice its size. This is called rising, and it takes
an hour or two. The holes in a slice of bread are where the bubbles
were.

Salt does more than add flavor to the bread. It slows the yeast down so
the dough does not rise too fast.

At last the baker shapes the dough and lets it rise once more. Then the
loaf goes into a hot oven, about four hundred degrees. The heat makes
the bubbles swell even bigger. Then it sets the dough firm around them.
The outside browns into a crust, and the kitchen fills with the smell.

V: knead = to push and fold dough again and again
V: yeast = a tiny living thing that makes dough rise
V: crust = the hard brown outside of a loaf
Q: What four things does a simple loaf need? | A simple loaf needs flour, water, salt, and yeast. | literal
Q: Why does a slice of bread have holes in it? | The holes are where bubbles of gas from the yeast were. | inference
Q: What does knead mean? | To knead means to push and fold dough again and again. | vocabulary
Q: What is this reading mostly about? | Yeast makes dough rise, and the oven bakes it into bread. | idea

# Bread Around the World

People in almost every land make bread, but not all bread looks alike.
Much of the bread in the world is flat. In Mexico, cooks pat corn dough
into thin round tortillas. In India, a flat wheat bread called chapati
puffs up on a hot pan. In Ethiopia, injera is a soft, spongy bread as
big as a tray. It is made from a tiny grain called teff and tastes a
little sour.

Other breads are tall loaves that rise with yeast. France is known for
the baguette, a long thin loaf with a crisp crust. In Germany and
Russia, many loaves are dark and made of rye.

Not all bread is made from wheat. In the American South, cornbread is
baked from ground corn. In southern India, cooks grind rice and lentils
into a batter. They steam it into soft white cakes called idli.

People have made bread for a very long time. Scientists found crumbs of
flat bread in a fire pit in Jordan. The crumbs were fourteen thousand
years old, older than farming itself. The ancient Egyptians baked loaves
with yeast more than four thousand years ago. Grain keeps well and is
cheap, and bread fills an empty stomach. The word companion once meant a
person you share bread with.

V: tortillas = thin flat round breads made from corn or wheat
V: rye = a grain like wheat that makes dark bread
V: ancient = from a very long time ago
